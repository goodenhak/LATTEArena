Here are two fixed decomposition examples adapted from successful feature-engineering patterns in the execution logs.

Example 1
Task: Predict whether a person should receive credit from tabular banking attributes such as checking status, duration, credit amount, purpose, and installment commitment.
Ordered subproblems:
1. Identify the original features that best describe repayment burden, loan size, and stable categorical context for credit approval.
2. Decide which simple transformations or feature combinations can capture repayment pressure or category-level priors, such as ratio, log, or group-based aggregation.
3. Generate the final candidate features in the required output format.

Example 2
Task: Predict whether a chemical is readily biodegradable from molecular descriptors such as oxygen count, nitrogen count, hydrogen-bond donors, spectral descriptors, and structural topology measures.
Ordered subproblems:
1. Identify descriptor groups that are most relevant to biodegradability, such as hetero-atom composition, hydrogen-bond capacity, and global structural descriptors.
2. Decide which interactions, ratios, differences, or nonlinear transforms are most likely to expose useful chemical patterns while remaining valid under the operator set.
3. Generate the final candidate features in the required output format.

Now decompose the current feature engineering task above into 2 to 3 ordered subproblems.
Each subproblem should be simpler than the full task and help the next one.
The last subproblem must ask for the final candidate features in the required output format.

Output requirements:
1. Return only the ordered subproblems.
2. One subproblem per line.
3. Do not answer the subproblems yet.
